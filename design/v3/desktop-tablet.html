Device
The form is split into 3 numbered groups so the eye moves top-to-bottom. Sex and priority are one-click choices, not dropdowns. Symptoms become tags, with common ones suggested. The right panel explains how it works and what a good question looks like, so a first-time user needs no training. ⌘↵ submits.
One focal point: the heartbeat line and a live checklist in plain language. The right panel repeats what you asked, so you can check it while you wait.
The answer comes first: the diagnosis in big type, with the confidence pill beside it. Recommendations are bullets with inline source tags. Hovering a tag highlights the matching source on the right. The actions sit at the bottom of the card, where you finish reading.
An amber banner explains in one sentence why caution is needed. "Suggest correction" opens inline, inside the card, so you never lose context. A "needs review" tag points at the claim that wasn't verified.
Safety rule: there is no confidence pill, and the red banner is unmissable. The diagnosis is shown as a "possible" assessment, and agreeing still requires a review.
After "I agree", a green confirmation replaces the buttons' focus and both dim, so you can't send twice. Copy flips to "Copied". The evidence panel is collapsed via the top-bar toggle, and the result gets the full width.
This is not a dead end. Your inputs are kept, you get one clear primary action (Try again), and you can go back and edit. The system-status link explains outages in plain words.
