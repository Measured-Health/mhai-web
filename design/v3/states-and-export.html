Errors appear when you press Analyse, not while typing. A summary at the top links to each problem, each field explains how to fix it in plain words, and focus jumps to the first error. Input is never cleared.
Skeletons match the real layout, so nothing jumps when content arrives. They're used when opening a past consultation, or while the sidebar history loads.
The first-time user gets a warm welcome and three example questions, not a blank screen. Clicking an example fills the form so they can see how it works.
Ward computers are shared. After 13 minutes idle, a countdown warns you, and at 15 you're signed out. A draft form is saved for this session, but never stored on the device.
Leaving a half-filled form asks first. "Keep editing" is the default (safe) action.
Three page-level problems in one view. Each says what happened and gives one clear way forward. Offline shows a dark strip at the top: the current result stays readable, and Analyse waits until you're back online.
Where you follow up on corrections you suggested. A notification appears when a reviewer decides, and the bell in the top bar keeps a history. Rejections always show the reviewer's reason.
One box to search consultations and run actions. It matches symptoms, diagnoses and specialties, and you can drive it with the arrow keys and Enter. On mobile it's the search field in the drawer.
Press ? anywhere. The shortcuts are grouped by where you use them.
Short, scannable help. It shows how to read a result, what each confidence level means, and a glossary that maps the words on screen to plain explanations.
"Copy" becomes a split button. The menu offers: copy as text (for the patient notes), print or save as PDF, and copy the ICD-10 code. Results that need review carry their red banner onto every export.
A4, black-and-white friendly. Confidence is shown as a word and bars, never colour alone. The sources are numbered, and the disclaimer and the reviewer line are in the footer. Only what the clinician typed is included; no names or IDs are added.
